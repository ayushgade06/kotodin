# Demo video script (~2:45)

**Before recording:** open `/program`, open the ⋯ menu → *Reset to demo program*. Use a 1440×900 window. Have a phone ready to scan a sticker.

| Time | Screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Landing hero, the two identical glasses | “These two glasses look the same. One filter is working. The other stopped weeks ago and is passing almost five times the WHO arsenic limit. Nobody can tell.” |
| 0:20–0:40 | Evidence strip (17.5 M, 95%, 3,534) | “Seventeen and a half million Bangladeshis drink water above the national arsenic standard. Household filters help, until maintenance stops: in one study 95% were abandoned within a year.” |
| 0:40–1:10 | `/program`: KPIs, *Visit this week*, map | “Kotodin gives every filter its own forecast. This is a demo program of forty synthetic households on real survey wells near Sonargaon. The queue tells the field worker who to visit first, and why.” |
| 1:10–1:40 | HH-014 → log effluent 14 µg/L → toast → *See the queue* (rows flash) | “The field worker tests HH-014’s filtered water: fourteen micrograms. Breakthrough. That’s the third one this program has caught, so Kotodin recalibrates: pouches are lasting about 30% less than the lab estimate. Three more households just moved into this week’s visits.” |
| 1:40–2:00 | Print sticker → scan with phone → Bangla card | “Each filter gets a sticker. The family scans it and sees, in Bangla, how many days are left. No app, no account, no personal data.” |
| 2:00–2:30 | `/lab`: drag pouch mass 250 → 400 g, then *Find the pouch size* | “For filter designers: the Design Lab runs a pouch design across all 3,534 wells in the national survey. At 250 grams, three in four households would need a new pouch every month. About 400 grams fixes that for 90% of them.” |
| 2:30–2:45 | `/methods` scroll, back to landing | “Every number is explained, and every assumption is labelled. Every filter runs out. Now we know when.” |
